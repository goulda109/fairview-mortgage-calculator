First-Time Buyer Programs at a Glance

FHSA — First Home Savings Account

Contribute up to $8,000/year ($40,000 lifetime). Contributions are tax-deductible like an RRSP, and qualifying withdrawals are completely tax-free like a TFSA. The single best first-home tool.

RRSP Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

Withdraw up to $60,000 per person ($120,000 per couple) from your RRSP tax-free for a first home. Repayable over 15 years, starting the 5th year after withdrawal.

First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit

Claim a $10,000 amount on your tax return the year you buy — worth $1,500 back as a non-refundable credit.

30-Year Insured Amortization

First-time buyers (and all new-build purchases) with an insured mortgage can amortize over 30 years instead of 25, lowering the monthly payment to qualify.

Land Transfer Tax Rebates

Ontario, BC, and PEI offer first-time buyer rebates on land transfer tax (e.g. up to $4,000 in Ontario). The Closing Costs tab applies these automatically.

GST/HST New Housing Rebate

Buying a new build? You may recover part of the GST/HST paid. Ask your Fairview broker — rules depend on price and province.

Good to Know

No Monthly Payments

Nothing is repaid until you sell, move out permanently, or the last homeowner passes away. You stay on title and stay in your home.

Tax-Free & Benefit-Safe

Proceeds are loan advances, not income — they're tax-free and don't reduce OAS or GIS benefits.

No-Negative-Equity Guarantee

Major Canadian providers guarantee you'll never owe more than the home's fair market value when it's sold, provided obligations are met.

The Trade-Off

Rates are higher than conventional mortgages and interest compounds, so the estate inherits less. The table above shows exactly how that plays out.
